Use cases we see in the field

Concrete problems, not “AI strategy”

Below are patterns we hear from local operators, backed by public reporting, trade research, and (where noted) vendor case studies. Your mileage varies; we validate numbers against your books before promising anything.

Medical & dental offices

  • No-shows and last-minute cancellations are a chronic revenue leak; national commentary often cites double-digit no-show rates for dental appointments.
  • Confirm/reschedule loops (SMS, voice, portal) and risk scoring for “likely no-show” slots are well-trodden wins. The point is execution tied to your PMS, not buying another generic chatbot.

Property management & real estate

  • Lease packets and PDFs into structured fields (dates, options, CAM) so portfolio reporting stops living in inboxes.
  • Maintenance triage: capture tenant issue → route → vendor. Industry write-ups describe large time savings when routine tickets stop being manually re-typed.
  • After-hours prospect questions (availability, tour booking) handled consistently without burning out a small leasing desk.

Restaurants & hospitality

  • Labor pressure is structural: trade surveys routinely show recruiting and retention among top operator concerns (see National Restaurant Association research).
  • Missed phone calls during service peaks translate to lost reservations; phone automation vendors publish eye-catching stats. We treat those as hypotheses until we see your call logs.

Professional & back office

  • Invoice / AP line extraction, contract comparison, and CRM hygiene (dedupe, enrichment) for teams that outgrew spreadsheets but aren’t ready for enterprise ERP rip-and-replace.
  • Internal Q&A over policy manuals and SOPs with citations, useful when onboarding staff or franchisees.

Further reading (external): dental scheduling & no-show discussion PatientDesk (industry blog); property-management cost / automation framing Conduit; restaurant call-handling benchmarks Loman (vendor). We don’t endorse every figure on those pages. We use them to start a grounded conversation.
